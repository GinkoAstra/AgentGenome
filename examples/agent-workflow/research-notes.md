## S1. Sunlight contains many wavelengths

Sunlight is a mixture of visible wavelengths. A prism or a rainbow separates
it into colours because each wavelength follows a slightly different path.

## S2. Small air molecules scatter short wavelengths more strongly

Air molecules are far smaller than the wavelength of visible light. For such
particles the scattering follows the Rayleigh regime, whose intensity is
proportional to one over the fourth power of the wavelength. Blue light
(about 450 nm) therefore scatters much more strongly than red light
(about 700 nm).

## S3. Scattered blue light arrives from every direction

When sunlight passes through the atmosphere, the blue part is redirected many
times. Some of that redirected light reaches an observer from directions away
from the Sun, so the sky looks blue. Violet scatters even more strongly, but
sunlight contains less violet and human eyes are less sensitive to it.

## S4. The setting Sun looks red because the light path is longer

Near sunrise or sunset, sunlight travels a much longer path through the
atmosphere. Most of the blue is scattered out of the direct beam, so the light
that still arrives directly is dominated by red and orange wavelengths.
